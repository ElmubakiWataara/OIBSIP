The Libyan leader whose four decades in power transformed his country and left a legacy that remains deeply contested.

01 / THE STORY

A military officer, revolutionary, political theorist, and the de facto leader of Libya for more than four decades.

Gaddafi was born near Sirte around 1942 and came of age during a period of powerful Arab nationalist movements. After military training, he joined fellow officers in a 1969 coup that removed King Idris and replaced the monarchy with a republic.

02 / BIOGRAPHY

A life that shaped the modern history of Libya.

Gaddafi became the central figure in Libya’s revolutionary government at the age of 27. His early programme fused Arab nationalism, state-led economic change, and a determination to control Libya’s oil wealth more directly. The government closed foreign military bases and brought more of the country’s oil industry under national control.

In the 1970s, he proposed the “Third International Theory,” later set out in The Green Book. It rejected both capitalism and communism, as well as representative democracy, and promoted a system of popular congresses and committees. In practice, political authority remained concentrated around Gaddafi and his revolutionary institutions.

His rule funded major social programmes and infrastructure while also facing severe criticism for repression, restrictions on independent political activity, and human-rights abuses. Libya’s relations with other countries shifted repeatedly, from periods of isolation and sanctions to renewed engagement in the 2000s.

In 2011, a mass uprising against his government became part of the First Libyan Civil War. Gaddafi was removed from power and killed near Sirte in October that year, ending a rule that had lasted from 1969 to 2011.

03 / KEY MOMENTS

A consequential timeline.

Five moments that help frame his political life and Libya’s changing course.

  1. c. 1942

    Born near Sirte

    Born into a family from the Qadhadhfa tribe in the central Libyan desert region.

  2. 1969

    The September Revolution

    Led fellow army officers in the coup that overthrew King Idris and ended Libya’s monarchy.

  3. 1977

    The Jamahiriya era

    Introduced a new state structure tied to his political theory of popular committees and congresses.

  4. 2003–04

    International re-engagement

    Libya agreed to dismantle weapons programmes, helping open a period of improved relations with Western governments.

  5. 2011

    Uprising and overthrow

    A nationwide revolt and civil war ended his government; he was killed near Sirte in October.

04 / REMEMBERING

To study Gaddafi is to study the complexities of modern Libya.

His supporters point to nationalism, state investment, and a forceful vision of African and Arab independence. His critics point to authoritarian rule, violence, and the suppression of dissent. A meaningful account must hold these realities together rather than reduce a nation’s history to a single story.
